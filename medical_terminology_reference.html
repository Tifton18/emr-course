How medical terms are built: Most medical words are constructed from Greek or Latin parts — a prefix (before the root), a root/combining form (the core meaning), and a suffix (after the root). Learning these parts lets you decode unfamiliar terms on the fly.

Memory Tip — Suffixes: "-itis" = inflammation (think "it is on fire"), "-oma" = tumor/mass, "-ology" = study of, "-ectomy" = cut it out, "-stomy" = open it up, "-tomy" = cut into it.
Decoding Unfamiliar Terms: Start at the end — identify the suffix first (tells you the type of condition), then the root (tells you the body part), then the prefix (tells you the qualifier). You can decode most terms you've never seen before.
Watch for Confusable Pairs:   Hyper- (too much) vs. Hypo- (too little)  |  Brady- (slow) vs. Tachy- (fast)  |  Apnea (no breath) vs. Dyspnea (difficult breathing)  |  Supine (face up) vs. Prone (face down)  |  Proximal (near trunk) vs. Distal (far from trunk)
